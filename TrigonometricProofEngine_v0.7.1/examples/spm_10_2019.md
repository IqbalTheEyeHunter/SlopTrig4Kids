# Trigonometric Proof Report — v0.7.1

**Status: PROVED**

$$\tan{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)} = \frac{1 - \cos{\left(x \right)}}{\sin{\left(x \right)}}$$

## Domain

- `cos(x/2) ≠ 0`
- `sin(x) ≠ 0`

## Algebraic proof

Start with the LHS:

$$\tan{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}$$

1. **Use the quotient or reciprocal identity**

$$= \frac{\sin{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}}{\cos{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}}$$

tan(x/2) = sin(x/2)/cos(x/2)

2. **Simplify the algebra**

$$= \frac{2 \sin^{2}{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}}{2 \sin{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)} \cos{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}}$$

Combine like terms and cancel common factors on the original domain.

3. **Use the double-angle identity**

$$= \frac{2 \sin^{2}{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}}{\sin{\left(x \right)}}$$

sin(2*u) = 2*sin(u)*cos(u), with u = x/2.

4. **Rearrange the algebra**

$$= \frac{1 - \left(1 - 2 \sin^{2}{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}\right)}{\sin{\left(x \right)}}$$

Use equivalent fractions, expansion or factorisation to write the expression in the required form.

5. **Use the double-angle identity**

$$= \frac{1 - \cos{\left(x \right)}}{\sin{\left(x \right)}}$$

cos(x) = 1 - 2*sin(x/2)**2.

This is the RHS, so the identity is proved on its original common domain.
