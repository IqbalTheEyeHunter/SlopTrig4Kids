# Trigonometric Proof Report — v0.7.1

**Status: PROVED**

$$- \tan{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)} + \cot{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)} = 2 \cot{\left(x \right)}$$

## Domain

- `cos(x/2) ≠ 0`
- `sin(x) ≠ 0`
- `sin(x/2) ≠ 0`

## Algebraic proof

Start with the LHS:

$$- \tan{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)} + \cot{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}$$

1. **Use the quotient or reciprocal identity**

$$= \frac{\left(-1\right) \sin{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}}{\cos{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}} + \cot{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}$$

tan(x/2) = sin(x/2)/cos(x/2)

2. **Use the quotient or reciprocal identity**

$$= \frac{\left(-1\right) \sin{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}}{\cos{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}} + \frac{\cos{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}}{\sin{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}}$$

cot(x/2) = cos(x/2)/sin(x/2)

3. **Simplify the algebra**

$$= \frac{2 \left(- \sin^{2}{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)} + \cos^{2}{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}\right)}{2 \sin{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)} \cos{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}}$$

Combine like terms and cancel common factors on the original domain.

4. **Use the double-angle identity**

$$= \frac{2 \left(- \sin^{2}{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)} + \cos^{2}{\left(\frac{x}{2} \right)}\right)}{\sin{\left(x \right)}}$$

sin(2*u) = 2*sin(u)*cos(u), with u = x/2.

5. **Use the double-angle identity**

$$= \frac{2 \cos{\left(x \right)}}{\sin{\left(x \right)}}$$

cos(x) = -sin(x/2)**2 + cos(x/2)**2.

6. **Rearrange the algebra**

$$= 2 \frac{\cos{\left(x \right)}}{\sin{\left(x \right)}}$$

Use equivalent fractions, expansion or factorisation to write the expression in the required form.

7. **Use the quotient or reciprocal identity**

$$= 2 \cot{\left(x \right)}$$

cot(x) = cos(x)/sin(x)

This is the RHS, so the identity is proved on its original common domain.
